# Contribution guidelines

The Wellcome Sanger Institute champions [open science](https://www.sanger.ac.uk/about/research-policies/open-access-science/), and we share this commitment.
GitHub is our primary platform for discussing and developing code.
Our tools are used by the wider community, and we welcome contributions that help us build and sustain an active development community.

We expect most code-related discussions to take place on GitHub.
Discussions may also refer to meetings and activities associated with biodiversity projects, such as [Darwin Tree of Life](https://www.darwintreeoflife.org/) and the [Vertebrate Genomes Project](https://vertebrategenomesproject.org/).

You are welcome to open issues and submit pull requests, provided you adhere to our [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).

Many of our repositories support production workflows, so maintaining their stability and functionality is a priority.
Maintainers will consider issues, proposed changes, and new contributions with these requirements in mind.

Please be patient when waiting for reviews or feedback. Our maintainers balance code development with other responsibilities, and responses may take time.

## AI Policy

We support using AI tools, including large language models (LLMs), to assist with coding.
However, you remain responsible for the code you submit, just as we remain responsible for the code we merge and release.

We expect you to understand the code you contribute and to be able to explain and justify your changes in your own words.
This applies to code, issues, pull request descriptions, and responses to review comments.
AI-generated code must be reviewed, understood, and tested by you before submission.
You should never submit code that you cannot explain or stand behind.
 
We expect all communication with maintainers and the community to reflect your own thoughts and understanding.
In particular, do not use an LLM to generate responses to questions or review comments from maintainers.
We want to hear what you think, not what an AI tool thinks on your behalf.
If you need help expressing yourself in English, using AI for language assistance is fine, provided the ideas and substance remain your own.

Code review is an opportunity for us to improve our software and for contributors to learn.
We are happy to help you understand our code and development practices, and we appreciate contributors who engage thoughtfully with feedback.
However, simply forwarding our review comments to an LLM and submitting its responses or resulting code is not meaningful engagement.

We want to interact with contributors who understand their contributions, can discuss their decisions, and are willing to learn from the review process.
We do not want to interact with someone acting merely as a proxy between maintainers and an AI tool.
We can use these tools ourselves; the value of a contribution lies in the understanding, judgement, and effort you bring to the process.

In summary:

- AI-assisted contributions are welcome. There is no blanket prohibition on AI-generated code, _provided you have reviewed, understood, and tested it yourself before submission_.
- Do not submit AI-generated pull requests that you cannot explain, justify, and take responsibility for.
- Do not use AI-generated responses as a substitute for communicating with maintainers. Questions and review comments must be addressed by the human contributor, in their own words.
- Code review is an opportunity to improve our software and for contributors to learn. We will not accept contributions without meaningful discussion and engagement with their _human_ authors.
- We reserve the right to warn contributors whose submissions or interactions fail to meet these expectations and, if necessary, to close their pull requests or stop engaging with them.
